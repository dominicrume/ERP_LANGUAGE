# Product & UX Rules — Steve Jobs Discipline

## The standing method
Define the ideal customer experience FIRST. Work backwards into the
architecture and code. Never the reverse. If a technical constraint fights
the experience, the constraint loses and gets re-engineered.

## The experience this product must deliver
1. A scenario reads like a short story, not a form. The narrative is the
   hero; inputs are decisions inside the story, not fields to fill.
2. A decision feels like turning over a card, not clicking submit. The
   consequence (score + plain-language reason) is a REVEAL, not a page
   reload or a toast notification.
3. Return visits feel like picking up where you left off — the product
   remembers the learner (see engineering-practices: persistent memory)
   and says so, visibly, on the very first screen.
4. Stickiness comes from mastery, not gimmicks: a visible best-score,
   a streak, and an honest "here's what tripped you up last time." No
   fake urgency, no dark patterns, no manufactured scarcity.
5. Instructors get a real authoring surface, not a config file exposed
   as a UI. If it looks like a database admin panel, it has failed.

## Non-negotiables (product admin)
6. Never claim a number the product cannot show working. If the pitch
   deck says "localized to 4 countries," the product must visibly
   demonstrate 4 distinct, correct outcomes on demand.
7. The learner's data (progress, mistakes) is never used for anything
   other than helping that learner. No cross-learner leakage, no
   secondary use, ever — stated plainly in-product, not buried in terms.
8. Every screen answers, within 3 seconds of looking at it: what is this,
   what do I do, what happens next. If it doesn't, redesign the screen —
   don't write a tooltip to explain it.
